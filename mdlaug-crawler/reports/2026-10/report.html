mDLAUG crawl · statistical review · run 4 (iPhone 13) · October 2026

Vendor Accessibility Signals

What an automated, phone-sized crawl of 132 digital-library sites can show with statistical confidence, broken down by platform vendor (90 sites whose own markup confirms the platform) and by type of institution. Anything the crawl could not determine automatically is left out of the findings and listed at the end as manual work.

Consent overlays are a vendor default, not a local choice. Every Digital Commons site opens a OneTrust cookie banner that Escape doesn't close. It isn't exposed as a dialog, and its only button is “Accept all cookies”: there is no way to reject or close it. 16 of 17 DSpace sites open the Klaro consent notice that ships with DSpace 7+. Escape doesn't close that one either, and on a third of those sites a keyboard user can't reach it within 10 Tab presses. No Omeka site and one CONTENTdm site (an institution's own content notice) open anything over the page.

Results pages split cleanly by vendor. None of 16 CONTENTdm results pages wraps its paging in a navigation landmark or marks the current page. DSpace does both on 9 of 10, and Omeka uses the landmark but never marks the current page. Search boxes sit in a search landmark on most CONTENTdm, Omeka and Samvera sites but on no DSpace or Digital Commons site. 7 of 17 DSpace home pages are wider than a phone screen.

Institution type still shows almost nothing. Public-library sites have more unnamed links, which is the one difference that holds up. The platform explains far more.

What we can't say yet. Digital Commons search results sit behind a Cloudflare bot check on 25 of 26 sites, so its results pages are unmeasured. Only 2 of the 16 sites listed as Islandora could be verified as running Islandora, so there are no Islandora claims. All findings use automated evidence only; no reviewer has checked the LLM judgments yet.

Platform differences

Each card shows the site-level value for each platform, from a phone-sized crawl (iPhone 13 emulation). A highlighted bar marked differs means that platform differs from the others at FDR q < 0.05. A finding is tiered Assert now when it passes every check:

  • significant after false-discovery correction across all 62 tests;
  • significant when platforms are compared only within the same type of institution;
  • still significant without Digital Commons, unless the claim is about Digital Commons itself;
  • the same platform ordering appears in run 1, an earlier desktop crawl of 125 sites (Spearman ρ ≥ 0.6); where that run has no such measure, q < 0.01 instead.

Anything significant that fails a check is shown as Supporting, with the failed check named. 12 findings are tiered "Assert now" and 1 "Supporting".

What would add the most

  1. Ask Elsevier/bepress to let the research crawler through. Digital Commons search is behind Cloudflare on 25 of 26 sites. An allowlist for the crawler's user agent, or a crawl from a university network, would make its results pages measurable. We won't solve or evade bot checks.
  2. Fix the Islandora sample. 14 of 16 listed “Islandora” sites carry no Islandora markers (many are custom Drupal or museum systems). A list of confirmed Islandora sites from the Islandora community would make that group usable.
  3. Calibrate the LLM checks with reviewers. Two people confirming about 30 findings each for alt-text quality, page structure and help pages would show which of those checks can be trusted. Each covers 50–130 sites.
  4. Try a larger local vision model for the screenshot check. It caught real crawler gaps in this run (a closed-shadow-DOM content notice and three bot-check pages, all fixed and re-crawled). It also calls share bars and chat buttons “cookie banners” (28 flags after dismissal, mostly those). A 12B model should cut the noise.
  5. Answer the institution question with pairs, not volume. The observed institution-type effects would need 40–60 sites per type. Comparing the same platform across institution types answers it more cheaply.

Method

Unit of analysis: one site. Primary sample: crawl run 4 (iPhone 13 emulation, 132 sites with a loaded home page; 90 of them vendor-verified). After crawler fixes, 20 sites were re-crawled (runs 5, 6) and those results replace run 4's for them. Replication sample: run 1 (desktop, 125 sites, 115 in both runs). Because the two runs mostly cover the same sites, replication shows the result isn't an artifact of the device or crawler version. It is not an independent sample. Pages that turned out to be bot-protection challenges are left out.

Evidence used: axe-core WCAG 2.2 A/AA rules, deterministic probes, and mDLAUG engine scorecards with confidence ≥ 0.7, not flagged for review, and not in an always-review situation (USE1, ACC3/COM4, FIL3/HEP1, RED4). LLM judgments were left out. Overlay measures come from the crawler's overlay pass, taken before each overlay was dismissed. Results-page measures cover only sites whose search reached results.

Tests: permutation Kruskal–Wallis (counts and scores) or chi-square (yes/no), 5000 permutations; a stratified permutation that shuffles platform only within library type (and the reverse); and, for platform, the same test without Digital Commons. Benjamini–Hochberg FDR runs across all 62 tests. Per-platform "differs" flags come from one-vs-rest Mann–Whitney or Fisher tests with their own FDR. Effect sizes are ε² (Kruskal–Wallis) or Cramér's V.

Reproduce with python scripts/significance.py then python scripts/significance_report.py. Per-site tables and every test are in data/significance/.
