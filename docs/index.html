mDLAUG · accessibility of digital libraries

mDLAUG Crawler

We visit digital-library websites the way a phone does, check them against the mobile Digital Library Accessibility and Usability Guidelines (mDLAUG), and measure which problems come from the platform vendor and which from the institution. This page collects the results, the data, and how to take part.

The October 2026 crawl in brief

  • Every Digital Commons site, and 16 of 17 DSpace sites, open a cookie-consent overlay that the Escape key doesn't close. The Digital Commons banner has no way to reject or close it at all.
  • CONTENTdm results pages never mark their page-number links as navigation or flag the current page; DSpace does both on 9 of 10 sites.
  • The type of institution (academic, public, museum…) explains far less than the platform it runs on.
  • Digital Commons search results sit behind a bot check, and most sites listed as Islandora couldn't be verified, so the report makes no claims about those.

Data

Spreadsheets open in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets. One row per site or per test.

FileWhat it holds
site_metrics_run4.csv Every measure for each of the 132 sites in the October crawl (one row per site)
tests.csv Every statistical test: measure, comparison, significance, effect size, tier
platform_labels.csv Each site's listed platform and what its own pages say it runs
manual_review_candidates.csv Checks that still need a person, with the number of sites and findings
site_metrics_run1.csv The same measures from the earlier desktop crawl, used to replicate the findings
October 2026 database Everything the crawler recorded, for the crawler's app. The training shows how to load it with one command.
